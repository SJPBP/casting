Introduction

Money sanctions have become an essential policy in international relations foreign policy and finance in this globalised world. It is basically the compelling of the state entities or individuals that are believed to be harmful to national or international security human rights and international order by governments or international organisations. Money sanctions can take many forms such as asset freezes trade embargos or prohibitions on certain financial transactions.

Policymakers legal practitioners and businesses must understand money sanctions. This paper covers the various types of money sanctions their governance under legal frameworks implications and future trends in this dynamic sector of finance.

Understanding Money Sanctions

Money sanctions are measures undertaken by governments or international bodies that impede restrict or even foreclose access to financial transactions resources and means of exchange. Generally the goal of such sanctions is to use economic power on particular individuals groups or countries. It forces them to alter certain behaviours or policies.

Types of Money Sanctions

Economic Sanctions

Economic sanctions usually mean stopping commercial exchange or financial transactions with a country or group of countries. These sanctions would be expected to weaken the economy of the targeted country and deprive it of the ability to compete in the world marketplace. Economic sanctions can include a wide range of activities such as

Bans on Importation and Exportation

Restricting the export and importation of certain goods or services to a targeted country.

Investment Bans

Prohibition of foreign investments in key sectors within a country.

Banking Bans

Access prohibition to banking in the world and international financial markets.

Financial Sanctions

Financial sanctions are restrictions on access to financial resources in general. The process of financial sanctioning can be

Asset Freezes

Freezing of funds held in a bank or by a financial institution.

Prohibition of Transactions

Prevention of prohibited persons or entities from conducting financial transactions.

Restrictive Investment

Ban financial institutions to invest in certain organisations or industries.

Sectoral Sanctions

Sectoral sanctions are those through which selective sectors within a country are targeted in an effort to influence those sectors that are critical to that country such as

Energy Sector

Provoking restrictions on export or import of oil and gas.

Defense Industry

Ban on exportation of military related goods and technologies.

Financial Sector

Ban on access to international finance or banking services. Targeted measures are intended to damage the economic infrastructure of a country without imposing total sanctions on its people.

Full Sanctions

Complete sanctions consist of much broader measures which are usually harsher measures that prohibit almost all trade and finance activities with a targeted country. These sanctions typically result from serious violations of international law or human rights. Complete sanctions can have a significant impact on the economy hence touching the bottom line of the ordinary citizen’s very livelihood.

Goals of Money Sanctions

The goals of money sanctions will vary according to the context in which they are created but are generally to

Behaviour Change

The main purpose of sanctions is to effect a change in behaviour compelling a targeted state or entity to reform often as it concerns matters that might include nuclear proliferation terrorism or human rights abuses.

Deterrence Sanctions act as deterrents to other states or entities. Sanctions apply an element of consequence for any violation of international norms and laws.

Human Rights Protection

Sanctions can protect human rights in the sense that they address regimes that commit egregious abuses or violations and give a word of condemnation as well as support for victims.

Promotion of International Stability

Applying pressure on rogue states or entities is therefore aimed at maintaining or restoring international peace and security.

Legal Framework Over Money Sanctions

United Nations Sanctions

The United Nations plays a pivotal role in the imposition of international sanctions by its Security Council. Under Chapter VII of the UN Charter the Security Council to maintain or restore international peace and security may impose sanctions of any of the following kinds

Economic Sanctions

Selectively on specific sectors or industries.

Arms Embargoes

Prohibition on arms sales and transfer to the country under sanction.

Travel Bans

Prohibit travel for identified individuals connected to the regime.

U.N. sanctions are a legal obligation to be implemented by all states in accordance with measures adopted by the Security Council.

Regional Sanctions

In addition to U.N. sanctions regional organisations like the E.U. A.U. and OAS might impose sanctions upon member states or entities. These can complement the U.N. sanctions or may respond to a particular regional concern for example

E.U. Sanctions

The E.U. has a structure for imposing sanctions which can include freezes on assets trade sanctions and travel bans. Such measures require agreement in the member states and are usually harmonised with U.N. sanctions.

African Union Sanctions

The A.U. can impose sanctions on member states violating democratic principles or human rights or violating peace agreements.

National Legislation

U.S. Sanctions Regime

The United States has one of the most complex sanctions regimes in the world and the OFAC mainly imposes that within the U.S. Department of the Treasury. U.S. sanctions can be divided into many different kinds such as

Country Specific Sanctions

Those imposed on countries such as Iran North Korea and Syria for their actions on terrorism nuclear proliferation or for human rights.

List Based Sanctions

Sanctions that are imposed on those listed in the Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list those with connections to terrorism drug trafficking and other illegal actions.

Secondary Sanctions

These wage battles against nonU.S. entities who engage in business activities with sanctioned countries thereby spreading U.S. sanctions worldwide.

European Union Sanctions

The European Union has its due laws on imposing sanctions and this is as per the regulations each of which is binding for the respective member states. Sanctions that the E.U. sets are E.U. typically based on one of the following

Asset Freezes

An asset freeze would mean no access to any financial assets. However they may be found within E.U. jurisdictions.

Trade Restrictions

Import or export of certain goods or services.

Due Diligence Requirements

Institutions and businesses have to maintain sanctions regulations very carefully through proper due diligence. These would include

Sanctions Screening

Periodic screening of transactions and customers against the list of sanctions for highlighting risks.

Know Your Customer (KYC)

It requires the proper installation of adequate KYC measures to understand the kind of relationship that exists with a customer and thus risk assessment.

Reporting Obligations

In general such institutions are obligated to report suspicious transactions or activities that indicate a potential breach of sanctions regulations.

In the event that one fails to observe sanctions they may face the main consequence or punishment that includes fines and reputational damage besides losing access to capital markets.

Economic Effects On Targeted Countries

Money sanctions are likely to have severe economic effects on the targeted country. Such money sanctions may lead to

Low Foreign Investment

Sanctions create uncertainty and risk which deters foreign investors from conducting business in the targeted country.

Low Trade

Import and export restrictions tend to distort trade relationships causing shortages of essential goods and services.

Devaluation of Currency

Sanctions tend to bring about decreased rates of a national currency which tend to increase the rates of inflation coupled with high costs of import commodities.

Increased Poverty

The economic depression resulting from the imposition of sanctions usually results in higher rates of unemployment and poverty. This affects innocent people more than the government being targeted.

Impact on Global Economy

Money sanctions can have wider impacts on the global economy as a whole where major economies or key commodities are involved. For instance

Price Fluctuations in Commodities

Sanctions against countries that are oil producing can trigger significant volatility in global oil prices due to supply chain and energy market ripple effects across the globe.

Sanctions on specific countries or regions can severely disrupt cross border companies with global supply chains and incur higher costs.

Legal and Regulatory Impact

The legal environment of a firm is also subject to cross border implications in terms of compliance under sanctions regimes. Some of these legal impacts are as follows

Secondary Sanctions Risk

The non U.S. entities are at risk of being caught up in secondary sanctions with activities involving doing business with sanctioned countries or individuals which would end up having a huge impact on their coffers.

Litigation

In the event that the business entity does not comply with the sanctions process the entity is likely to face lawsuits regulatory actions and reputation damage.

Regulatory Scrutiny

With regulators becoming more scrutinising the compliance programs and practices adopted by companies the burden of compliance is only increased.

Social Implications

Whereas the money sanctions direct themselves on certain specific behaviours or entities they often target other social effects which include

Humanitarian Crises

Sanctions that result in drastic shortages of food medicines and essentials cause severe harm to vulnerable populations.

Public Resentment

Sanctions can provoke public resentment against the targeted government but can also generate sympathy for the regime among citizens who feel the sanctions are unjust.

Political Costs

Money sanctions can cause extreme geopolitical tension and diplomatic crises during their implementation. The significant political costs are

Retaliation Countries sanctioned in response may retaliate by imposing retaliatory measures thus entering into an endless cycle of more tensions and conflict.

Realignment of Alliances

Countries that are sanctioned may embark on strengthening ties and alliances with non western allies or generating alternative trade partnerships to counter the sanctions.

Role of Financial Institutions

Compliance and Risk Management

Financial institutions play a core role in the implementation and application of money sanctions. In this regard they have 

Screening Against Sanctions

They are obliged to regularly screen transactions and customer activities against government issued sanctions lists in order to establish potential risks.

Risk Assessments

Conduct deep risk assessments on customers transactions and geographical locations to identify any possible exposure to sanctions.

Reporting Obligations

Sanctions institutions are generally liable to report suspicious activities or transactions that may be a breach of the sanctions regulations to the pertinent authorities.

Technology and Automation

Increasing complexity in sanctions compliance has made most financial institutions opt for more advanced technologies and automated systems. These assist in

Real Time Monitoring

Leverage automated systems for real time transaction monitoring and alerts for activities that appear possibly suspicious.

Data Analytics

Data analytics is used to track patterns and trends of customer behaviour and improve compliance in such ways.

Artificial Intelligence

Applying A.I. solutions to sanctions screening processes would give fair accuracy and significantly improve efficiency thereby derisking false positives.

Challenges and Best Practices

Challenges and best practices in sanctions compliance for financial institutions

Dynamic Nature of Sanctions

Sanctions constantly evolve and institutions need to be updated on the regulatory position and adapt their compliance programs accordingly.

Geographical Risks

Institutions operating in different jurisdictions have to adhere to varying sanction regimes and compliance obligations thereby adding to the overall complexity of operations.

Resource Constraints

Sanctions compliance is typically under resourced and inadequate monitoring and enforcement do happen in quite a few institutions. Best practices for financial institutions are as follows

Regular Training

Ongoing training should be provided to the staff of financial institutions on sanctions compliance risk management and exposure to consequences of non compliance.

Cooperation

With industry peers regulatory bodies and law enforcement to share insights and good practices about sanctions compliance.

Robust Audit Processes

Carrying out regular audits and assessments in compliance programs to identify weaknesses and rectify them so that an effective compliance program can be ensured.

Case Studies of Money Sanctions

Iran Sanctions

Iran has been sanctioned on a massive scale by the U.S. and U.S. allies primarily on the basis of its nuclear programme and its track record of patronising terrorism. The sanctions regime is composed of all pervasive economic sanctions barring trade in strategic sectors such as energy finance and technology.

Asset freezes

Iranian assets situated abroad are under seizure.

Trading restrictions

The import and export of specific goods and services are prohibited. These sanctions have been quite illustrative such as

Economic Contraction

Iran’s economy has contracted coupled with galloping inflation currency devaluation and decreased foreign investment.

Humanitarian Impact

The sanctions have resulted in shortages of medicines and food products spreading misery to other sections of Iranian society.

Russia Sanctions

Russia for annexing Crimea in 2014 and inciting the Ukraine conflict retaliated with enhanced comprehensive sanctions targeting various critical sectors of the Russian economy in unison with the United States E.U. and other countries.

Other measures included

Sectoral Sanctions

Focus on cutting off Russia’s access to international financing and technology by focusing on energy finance and defence.

Asset Freezes

Freeze the assets of key individuals and entities connected with the government of Russia. These sanctions have had the following implications such as

Economic Isolation

Sanctions have made Russia more economically isolated than ever as spectacular declines have been witnessed in foreign investment and overall economic growth.

Currency Volatility

The Russian ruble has become volatile considering the sanctions and a lower foreign exchange reserve.

Sanctions against North Korea

In the case of North Korea a comprehensive set of all around sanctions has been imposed on it owing to its nuclear weapons program and missile tests. The sanctions system comprises of

Economic Sanctions

Halt exportation activities especially in the coal and textile sectors which are the primary sources of current revenue for the North Korean government.

Financial Sanctions

Deprive North Korea of access to international financial systems. These sanctions effects have been described by

Humanitarian Crisis

These sanctions have created levels of poverty and hunger and humanitarian organisations are wondering about the critical situations north koreans face.

Proving with Missiles

This country keeps testing missiles. At the same time in a sanctioned position it makes one wonder about the instrument of foreign policy sanctions and how future money sanctions trends will be part of the greater use of targeted sanctions.

Future Money Sanctions Trends

Deeper Targeted Sanctions

This trend of targeted or smart sanctions is certainly likely to continue because policymakers want to minimise the negative spillover on the civilian population while exerting increased pressure on regimes or entities.

Targeted sanctions are more effective in achieving certain political or social objectives with much less suffering.

Stepped Up International Cooperation

Sanction enforcement becomes very important with increased inter connectivity and inter dependence in the financial system. As such more inter connectedness is going to make people see more cooperation concerning information sharing best practices compliance and enforcement by counties.

Increased interconnection will improve the effectiveness of sanctions and reduce risks of evasion.

Technological Changes

Technology changes will shape the landscape of sanctions.

Artificial Intelligence

The deployment of A.I. Machine learning can facilitate the imposition of better accuracy in transaction monitoring and reduce false positives.

Blockchain Technology

Blockchain technology can elevate the transparency and traceability components of financial transactions making it more challenging for sanctioned entities to circumvent controls imposed on them.

Changing Geopolitics

Political geography is constantly evolving which in turn will determine the use and effectiveness of monetary sanctions. The emerging new powers or the new relationships in international relations are expected to change the dynamics of sanctions therefore governments and financial institutions must continuously adjust to these changes.

Conclusion

Money sanctions have gradually become the nucleus instrument that governments and international organisations deploy to influence behaviour keep the peace and protect human rights. However though carrying extremely important objectives the application of sanctions results in very powerful economic legal social and political consequences.

Sanctions can have severe implications on targeted countries including economic decline humanitarian crises and upsurging poverty rates. Therefore well developed compliance programs and associated risk management practices are necessary for the implementation of sanctions in financial institutions.
